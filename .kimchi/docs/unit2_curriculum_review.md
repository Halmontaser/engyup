## Executive Summary

This review analyzed **629** activities across **88** lessons in Unit 2. A total of **468** missing-content gaps were identified, and many lessons show activity ordering that does not progress from easy to difficult.

### Top findings

- **Audio gaps are the most common issue:** many listening/speaking activities lack audio files.
- **Missing images:** visual support is absent from many activities where it would aid Arabic-speaking learners.
- **Questions are too sparse:** several comprehension activities have fewer than 3 questions.
- **Ordering often starts too hard:** some lessons begin with high-demand activities before learners have received vocabulary or listening input.
- **Production tasks lack models:** transform-sentence, picture-description, and conversation-sim activities frequently lack example/model answers.

## Priority Recommendations

### Immediate (high impact, low effort)
1. **Add audio files** to all listening-comprehension, pronunciation-practice, dialogue-read, and dictation activities that are missing them.
2. **Add transcripts** to listening-comprehension activities to support Arabic-speaker decoding.
3. **Expand question banks** to at least 3–5 items per comprehension activity.
4. **Add example/model answers** to transform-sentence, picture-description, and conversation-sim activities.

### Short-term (medium effort)
5. **Add relevant images** to flashcard, reading-passage, and picture-description activities.
6. **Reorder activities** within lessons to follow the Input → Guided Practice → Application → Production progression.
7. **Add warm-up flashcards** at the start of lessons that currently open with hard activities.

### Long-term (structural)
8. **Standardize difficulty labels** (`beginner`/`intermediate`/`advanced`) to match the heuristic scores.
9. **Introduce pre-reading/pre-listening tasks** before main comprehension activities.
10. **Review culturally-specific examples** for Arabic-speaking learners in Yemen/Gulf contexts.
